Alejandro de la Fuente-Cuesta

Large language models · political information · online incivility

PhD researcher in computational social science at the Barcelona Supercomputing Center and the University of Barcelona.

I specialise in large language models as a research method for the social sciences: using them to measure political content at a scale human coding cannot reach, validating what they produce against human judgement, and building experiments in which participants interact with LLM-driven agents.

I apply these methods to how people find political information online and what they do with it. Most of my work links passive web-tracking data with surveys of the same panelists, which lets me compare what people say they read with what their browsers actually record.

Human or AI? Play the collective Turing test

Game · 6 random threads from the study, 3 human and 3 AI

Read short Reddit-style discussions about climate change and immigration and guess whether real people or our multi-agent AI system wrote them. After each guess you see the answer and how the 643 participants in our study judged the same thread. They got 49% right.

Beyond the Link: Assessing LLMs’ Ability to Classify Political Content across Global Media

with Alberto Martinez-Serra, Nienke Visscher, Laia Castro and Ana S. Cardenal · arXiv, 2025

Web-tracking studies usually record URLs, not the articles behind them. We test whether open-weight LLMs can tell political from non-political news across five countries, and find that the URL alone is often as informative as the full text, with balanced accuracy up to 92% against human coders. Errors are not spread evenly: agreement falls to about 65% for ideologically centrist articles and approaches 100% at the extremes.

STAGELab: a chat platform for experiments on online incivility

Software · WHAT-IF project

Participants discuss a news story in a group chat with LLM-driven agents. The share of uncivil and like-minded messages is set by the experimental condition, so we can see how tone and agreement change what people say, report and block.
